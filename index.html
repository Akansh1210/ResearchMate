METHODOLOGY PLANNER

Plan your research methodology

These prompts help you think through design choices. They do not automatically determine the correct method.

1. Research approach

Quantitative, qualitative or mixed methods — choose based on the research question and the kind of evidence needed.

2. Research design

Examples include descriptive survey, case study, experiment, correlational study, phenomenology or design-based research.

3. Participants and sampling

Define the target population, inclusion criteria, sampling strategy and a justified sample size.

4. Data sources and instruments

Consider questionnaires, interviews, observations, tests, documents or digital learning records.

5. Analysis plan

Specify how each research question will be addressed and how data quality will be checked.

DATA ANALYSIS LEARNING GUIDE

Choose an analysis pathway

Quantitative data

Begin with data cleaning and descriptive statistics (frequencies, percentages, mean and standard deviation). Depending on the question and assumptions, inferential methods may include t-tests, chi-square tests, correlation or regression.

Qualitative data

Become familiar with the data, develop codes, group codes into categories or themes, review interpretations and support claims with appropriate evidence. Reflexivity and a transparent audit trail matter.

Tools to learn

SPSS can support statistical analysis. MAXQDA can support qualitative and mixed-methods data organisation and analysis. Software does not replace methodological reasoning.

Before you analyse

Match the analysis to each research question, understand assumptions, document decisions and avoid claiming causation from designs that cannot establish it.
